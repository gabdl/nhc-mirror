For the central North Pacific...between 140W and 180W:

1. Eastern Central Pacific (CP95):
Showers and thunderstorms associated with an area of low pressure
located several hundred miles to the southeast of the Big Island of

Tropical Weather Outlook
NWS National Hurricane Center Miami FL
400 PM PST Thu Nov 5 2026

For the eastern and central North Pacific east of 180 longitude:

1. Eastern Central Pacific (CP95): 
Showers and thunderstorms associated with an area of low pressure 
located several hundred miles to the southeast of the Big Island of 
Hawaii continue to show signs of organization, though recent 
satellite wind data indicate the system lacks a well-defined center. 
Environmental conditions are conducive for additional development, 
and a tropical depression is expected to develop by early Friday. 
The system is forecast to move west-northwestward to westward at 10 
to 15 mph, passing well south of the Hawaiian Islands. 
* Formation chance through 48 hours...high...90 percent.
* Formation chance through 7 days...high...90 percent.

2. Eastern East Pacific:
A broad area of low pressure offshore of Central America is 
producing a large area of showers and thunderstorms that remain 
disorganized. Gradual development of this system is still possible 
during the next couple of days, and a tropical depression could 
form by this weekend while the low drifts slowly offshore of the 
Central America coast. Regardless of development, this system could 
bring areas of heavy rain to portions of Central America through 
this weekend.
* Formation chance through 48 hours...medium...40 percent.
* Formation chance through 7 days...medium...40 percent.



Forecaster Evans/Blake

Para el Pacífico Norte oriental y central al este de 180 longitud

Pacífico Central Este (CP95): Lluvias y tormentas eléctricas
asociadas con un área de baja presión ubicada varios cientos de
millas al sureste de la Isla Grande de Hawái continúan mostrando
signos de organización, aunque datos recientes de viento por
satélite indican que el sistema carece de un centro bien definido.
Las condiciones ambientales son propicias para un desarrollo
adicional, y se espera que se desarrolle una depresión tropical para
la primera hora del viernes. Se pronostica que el sistema se
desplazará del oeste-noroeste a oeste a 10 a 15 mph, pasando bien al
sur de las Islas Hawaianas.
* Probabilidad de formación hasta 48 horas...alta...90 por ciento.
* Probabilidad de formación hasta 7 días...alta...90 por ciento.

Este del Pacífico: Una amplia área de baja presión hacia el mar de
Centroamérica está produciendo una gran área de aguaceros y
tormentas eléctricas que permanecen desorganizadas. Todavía es
posible el desarrollo gradual de este sistema durante los próximos
dos días, y una depresión tropical podría formarse para este fin de
semana a medida que las deriva bajas lentamente hacia el mar de la
costa de Centroamérica. Independientemente del desarrollo, este
sistema podría traer áreas de lluvia intensa a porciones de
Centroamérica hasta este fin de semana.
* Probabilidad de formación hasta 48 horas...medio...40 por ciento.
* Probabilidad de formación hasta 7 días...medio...40 por ciento.

Tropical Weather Outlook
NWS National Hurricane Center Miami FL
700 PM EST Thu Nov 5 2026

For the North Atlantic...Caribbean Sea and the Gulf of America:

1. Western and Central Atlantic:
A gale-force, non-tropical low pressure area located a few hundred 
miles south of Bermuda is producing disorganized convection. 
Environmental conditions could allow this system to acquire some 
subtropical characteristics during the next couple of days while it 
moves slowly eastward over the western and central subtropical 
Atlantic. By late this weekend, upper-level winds are forecast to 
become unfavorable for further development. While a new non- 
tropical low could form from the remnants of the current low or to 
its east early next week, environmental conditions are unlikely to 
support significant development of that system. Regardless, 
gale-force winds are likely over parts of the central subtropical 
Atlantic through the weekend. For more information, including Gale 
Warnings, please see High Seas Forecasts issued by the National 
Weather Service.
* Formation chance through 48 hours...low...30 percent.
* Formation chance through 7 days...medium...40 percent.



High Seas Forecasts issued by the National Weather Service can be 
found under AWIPS header NFDHSFAT1, WMO header FZNT01 KWBC, and 
online at ocean.weather.gov/shtml/NFDHSFAT1.php


Forecaster Evans/Blake
