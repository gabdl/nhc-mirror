ZCZC HFOTWOCP ALL
TTAA00 PHFO DDHHMM

Tropical Weather Outlook
NWS Central Pacific Hurricane Center Honolulu HI
Issued by NWS National Hurricane Center Miami FL
800 PM HST Sun Aug 30 2026

For the central North Pacific...between 140W and 180W:

Active Systems:
The National Hurricane Center is issuing advisories on Hurricane
Karina, located well west-southwest of the southern tip of the Baja
California Peninsula, and on Tropical Storm Lowell, located well
east-southeast of the Hawaiian Islands.

1. Central Pacific:
An area of low pressure could form well south or southwest of the
Hawaiian Islands in a couple of days. Some gradual development is
possible thereafter while it moves slowly westward across the
central Pacific.
* Formation chance through 48 hours...low...near 0 percent.
* Formation chance through 7 days...low...30 percent.



Forecaster Gibbs

Tropical Weather Outlook
NWS National Hurricane Center Miami FL
1100 PM PDT Sun Aug 30 2026

For the eastern and central North Pacific east of 180 longitude:

Active Systems:
The National Hurricane Center is issuing advisories on Hurricane 
Karina, located well west-southwest of the southern tip of the Baja 
California Peninsula, and on Tropical Storm Lowell, located well 
east-southeast of the Hawaiian Islands.

1. Well Offshore of Southwestern Mexico (EP95):
Showers and thunderstorms associated with a broad area of low 
pressure located well offshore of southwestern Mexico remain 
disorganized. However, environmental conditions appear conducive for 
gradual development, and a tropical depression is expected to form 
within the next couple of days while the system continues to move 
west-northwestward to northwestward at 10 to 15 mph.
* Formation chance through 48 hours...high...90 percent.
* Formation chance through 7 days...high...90 percent.

2. Central Pacific:
An area of low pressure could form well south or southwest of the 
Hawaiian Islands in a couple of days. Some gradual development is 
possible thereafter while it moves slowly westward across the 
central Pacific.
* Formation chance through 48 hours...low...near 0 percent.
* Formation chance through 7 days...low...30 percent.



Forecaster Gibbs

ZCZC MIATWOAT ALL
TTAA00 KNHC DDHHMM

Tropical Weather Outlook
NWS National Hurricane Center Miami FL
200 AM EDT Mon Aug 31 2026

For the North Atlantic...Caribbean Sea and the Gulf of America:

1. Northern Gulf of America (AL97):
An area of low pressure over the northern Gulf of America, located 
more than 100 miles south of the coast of southern Louisiana  
continues to produce a small but concentrated area of showers and 
thunderstorms. Earlier satellite wind data indicated that the 
circulation has not become much better defined from yesterday while 
producing winds of 30 to 35 mph. Regardless, additional development 
is anticipated as the low moves slowly west-northwestward, and a 
short-lived tropical depression or tropical storm is likely to form 
before the system reaches the Upper Texas or southwestern Louisiana 
coasts by tonight or Tuesday morning.

Interests along the southwest Louisiana and Upper Texas coasts 
should monitor the progress of this system, as tropical storm 
watches or warnings could be required for portions of the area later 
today. Regardless of development, this system is expected to bring 
locally heavy rainfall to portions of the Upper Texas and 
southwestern Louisiana coasts during the next several days. An Air 
Force Reserve reconnaissance aircraft is scheduled to investigate 
the system later this morning. Additional information on this 
system, including gale warnings, can be found in High Seas Forecasts 
issued by the National Weather Service.
* Formation chance through 48 hours...high...70 percent.
* Formation chance through 7 days...high...70 percent.

2. Near Puerto Rico and the Virgin Islands (Remnants of Dolly):
The remnants of Tropical Storm Dolly, a surface trough of low 
pressure, continues to produce an area of showers and thunderstorms 
from Hispaniola eastward to the northern Leeward Islands. Strong 
upper-level winds are expected to prevent redevelopment of this 
system over the next day or two while it moves quickly westward to 
west-northwestward at 20 to 25 mph. Conditions could become more 
conducive for redevelopment by the middle of the week as the system 
reaches the southern Bahamas or Florida Straits. Regardless, the 
system could bring locally heavy rains and gusty winds to portions 
of the Leeward Islands, the Virgin Islands, Puerto Rico, and 
Hispaniola over the next couple of days.
* Formation chance through 48 hours...low...near 0 percent.
* Formation chance through 7 days...low...20 percent.



High Seas Forecasts issued by the National Weather Service
can be found under AWIPS header NFDHSFAT1, WMO header FZNT01 
KWBC, and online at ocean.weather.gov/shtml/NFDHSFAT1.php


Forecaster Papin
