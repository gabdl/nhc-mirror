For the central North Pacific...between 140W and 180W:

Active Systems:
The National Hurricane Center is issuing advisories on Hurricane
Polo, located about 150 miles offshore of southwestern Mexico, on
Hurricane Odalys, located well west-southwest of the southern tip of
Baja California, and on Tropical Storm Nolo, located a few hundred
miles south of the Big Island of Hawaii.

1. Well East-Southeast of the Hawaiian Islands:
An area of low pressure could form about 1000 miles east-southeast
of the Hawaiian Islands over the weekend. Environmental conditions
could support some gradual development of this system, and a
tropical depression could form late this weekend or early next week.
The system is forecast to move generally northeastward, remaining
well east-southeast of the Hawaiian Islands.
* Formation chance through 48 hours...low...near 0 percent.
* Formation chance through 7 days...medium...40 percent.

NWS National Hurricane Center Miami FL
500 PM PDT Thu Sep 24 2026

For the eastern and central North Pacific east of 180 longitude:

Active Systems:
The National Hurricane Center is issuing advisories on Hurricane 
Polo, located about 150 miles offshore of southwestern Mexico, on 
Hurricane Odalys, located well west-southwest of the southern tip of 
Baja California, and on Tropical Storm Nolo, located a few hundred 
miles south of the Big Island of Hawaii.

1. South of the Gulf of Tehuantepec:
An area of low pressure is expected to form in a couple of days to 
the south of the Gulf of Tehuantepec. Afterwards, environmental

northwestward, parallel to the coast of southern and southwestern 
Mexico.

* Formation chance through 7 days...high...80 percent.

of the Hawaiian Islands over the weekend. Environmental conditions 
could support some gradual development of this system, and a 
tropical depression could form late this weekend or early next week.

well east-southeast of the Hawaiian Islands.

Tropical Weather Outlook
NWS National Hurricane Center Miami FL
800 PM EDT Thu Sep 24 2026

For the North Atlantic...Caribbean Sea and the Gulf of America:

Active Systems:
The National Hurricane Center is issuing advisories on Tropical 
Storm Fay, located several hundred miles southwest of the Azores.

1. Far Eastern Tropical Atlantic (AL90):
Recent satellite-derived wind data indicates that an area of low 
pressure located less than 200 miles to the south-southeast of the 
Cabo Verde Islands is producing winds of 40-45 mph to the north of 
its center, which is gradually becoming better defined. In addition, 
the system is producing a large area of showers and thunderstorms 
that are also showing signs of organization. If these current trends 
continue, the system is likely to become a short-lived tropical 
storm as soon as later tonight or on Friday while it moves 
northwestward to northward. Increasing upper-level winds, however, 
should prevent further development over the weekend. Interests in 
the Cabo Verde Islands should monitor the progress of this system as 
gusty winds, areas of heavy rain, and flash flooding are possible 
over the islands by Friday.  Heavy rain and flash flooding could 
also occur well to the east along the coast of western Africa, 
particularly in western portions of Senegal, The Gambia, 
Guinea-Bissau, and southwest portions of Mauritania.
* Formation chance through 48 hours...high...80 percent.
* Formation chance through 7 days...high...80 percent.



Forecaster Papin

Para el Atlántico Norte...Mar Caribe y el Golfo de América

Sistemas activos: El Centro Nacional de Huracanes está emitiendo
advertencias sobre la Tormenta Tropical Fay, ubicada a varios
cientos de millas al suroeste de las Azores.

Lejano Oriente Atlántico Tropical (AL90): Datos recientes de viento
derivados de satélite indican que un área de baja presión ubicada a
menos de 200 millas al sur-sureste de las Islas de Cabo Verde está
produciendo vientos de 40-45 mph al norte de su centro, que se está
definiendo mejor. Además, el sistema está produciendo una gran área
de aguaceros y tormentas eléctricas que también están mostrando
signos de organización. Si estas tendencias actuales continúan, es
probable que el sistema se convierta en una tormenta tropical de
corta duración tan pronto como esta noche o el viernes, a medida que
se mueve de noroeste a norte. Sin embargo, el aumento de los vientos
de nivel superior debería evitar un mayor desarrollo durante el fin
de semana. Las personas interesadas en las Islas de Cabo Verde deben
monitorear el progreso de este sistema, ya que es posible que se
produzcan ráfagas de viento, áreas de lluvia intensa e inundaciones
repentinas sobre las islas para el viernes. Fuertes lluvias e
inundaciones repentinas también podrían ocurrir al este a lo largo
de la costa de África occidental, particularmente en las porciones
occidentales de Senegal, Gambia, Guinea-Bissau y porciones del
suroeste de Mauritania.
